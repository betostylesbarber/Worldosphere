## 2. What the reference does well, and what Worldosphere does differently
**MEASURED and OBSERVED** (04, 10):
- The reference makes survival *logic* entertaining:
  - a concrete premise in frame 0
  - invisible mechanisms drawn as maps and diagrams
  - a ladder of small payoffs
  - silence as punctuation (music dropping to about −50 dBFS)
  - dense, short captions (about 3 words for about 1 s)
  - a −14 LUFS mix.
- Its weaknesses:
  - a false water claim (73 gal "for weeks" for a herd that needs about 110–183 gal a day)
  - a doubtful 3-mile audibility claim
  - caption/speech mismatches
  - an ambiguous speaker moment
  - time-of-day jumps
  - endings that resolve only if you follow.

**Worldosphere keeps the principles and replaces the execution** (08, 09, 10):
- **Direction A, "The Fork", chosen** over "Field Notes" (too slow) and "Outbreak Atlas" (drifts into geography).
- **Promise:** *"Worldosphere drops you into the first minutes of a disaster — zombie or real — and shows the one decision that decides who makes it."*
- **Three signatures:**
  - **THE FORK:** the frame freezes and splits on an amber crack to show both options.
  - **THE SPHERE:** colour-coded domes make sound, water, threat and gas visible.
  - **THE VERDICT:** every episode resolves on a paper card with a status, the decisive factor and, for real disasters, a fact-checked REAL RULE.
- **An original cast:** Kai (17), Mia (8), Tess (32, delivery driver) and Owen (64, retired lineman), plus "the Turned", who don't breathe, push but can't pull, and can't climb, swim or work handles.
- **An original "inked diorama" style and type system:** Big Shoulders Display, Atkinson Hyperlegible Next and JetBrains Mono, all under the SIL OFL; captions sit in lanes clear of the Shorts UI.
- **Three pillars:**
  - P1 OUTBREAK (14 episodes; fiction under fixed world rules)
  - P2 REAL RULE (13; real emergencies, with every claim in a 71-row claim ledger)
  - P3 WORLD SHIFT (3).

## 4. How to make each episode
- **The workflow** (12) has 17 stages, from topic selection to performance review. Each has inputs, actions, tools, outputs, acceptance criteria, failure fixes and handoffs.
- **Toolchain:**
  - Primary: Illustrator or Affinity → After Effects + Duik (or Moho) → DaVinci Resolve.
  - Free fallback: Inkscape → Blender Grease Pencil → Resolve free, plus Subtitle Edit.
- **Format:** 1080×1920 at 24 fps, H.264 High, AAC 48 kHz, −14 LUFS ±1 and ≤ −1 dBTP (a convention, not a YouTube rule).
- **Templates and checks** (13): templates for every stage, and a master QA checklist with hard stops. A video passes playback review only after a full viewing with sound, a muted viewing and a phone check **of the exported file**.
- **One page to work from:** `24_Make_the_Next_Worldosphere_Video_Checklist.md`.

**Capacity, stated plainly** (12 §9, 20 §2):
- 30 consecutive daily episodes at this quality need an **estimated ≈15 focused hours per publishing day**. One person cannot sustain that.
- **Recommendation: two people** (an animator and a producer) for the 31 Oct launch.
- With one person, use the **lean scope** and the 9 Nov launch, or launch later, or publish 3–4 episodes a week. Never lower the QA bar.
- Days 1–3 serve as time pilots, so the decision at the gate uses measured hours, not guesses.

## 6. How to improve using real results

- **Metrics with denominators:** engaged views as the base; "Stayed to watch" for viewing choice; average percentage viewed is not a completion rate.
- **12 tests** (T-01 to T-12): hooks, pillars, cast, script density, captions, clarity, sound, Fork timing, CTA, duration and posting time, Both Endings, saga. Each has a hypothesis, measure, guardrail, comparison, window and decision rule.
