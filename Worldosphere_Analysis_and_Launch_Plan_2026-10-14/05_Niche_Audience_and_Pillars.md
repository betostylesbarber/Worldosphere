## 3. Worldosphere audience promise (RECOMMENDED)

> **"Worldosphere drops you into the first minutes of a disaster — zombie or real — and shows the one decision that decides who makes it."**

Short form for the bio, banner and pinned comments: **"One scenario. One decision. See who makes it."**

What a returning viewer gets, which is the subscriber benefit:
1. **A new scenario every day**, each one complete in under 90 seconds.
2. **A decision you can argue about.** The Fork shows both options.
3. **Something real to keep.** Real-disaster episodes end with a fact-checked REAL RULE, and outbreak rules stay consistent from episode to episode.
4. **A cast you recognise.** Kai, Mia, Tess and Owen appear in different scenarios. Each scenario stands alone.

## 4. Content pillars (a small, coherent set)

| Pillar | Definition | Viewer job | Share of the 30-day plan | Tone |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **P1 — OUTBREAK** | Zombie / infected scenarios in specific everyday places, under fixed Worldosphere outbreak rules (07) | Suspense + "what would I do" | 14 / 30 | Tense, clever, occasionally wry |
| **P2 — REAL RULE** | Real disasters and emergencies (water, fire, quake, weather, ice, crowd) as a 60-second story. Every safety claim fact-checked | Suspense + useful knowledge + share-to-protect | 13 / 30 (9 of them depend on a BLOCKING claim being verified first: WS-002, 005, 012, 018, 023, 025, 027, 028, 029; verified reserves R01, R02, R08 stand by) | Grounded, urgent, never preachy |
| **P3 — WORLD SHIFT** | Grid-down and end-of-the-world hypotheticals (blackouts, solar storms) and "Both Endings" decision labs | Big-picture curiosity, debate | 3 / 30 (the experimental pillar) | Speculative but rule-bound |

Cross-cutting device, used in all pillars: **the decision.** Every episode turns on one visible choice (the Fork) and ends on a stated outcome (the Verdict).

### Pillar boundaries (keep / avoid)
- **Keep:** ordinary places, physical cause and effect, consequences, small surprises, ensemble cast, clean animation, accurate facts.
- **Avoid:**
  - Gore close-ups and combat-as-spectacle (age-restriction risk; off-brand).
  - Real recent tragedies with identifiable victims.
  - Fake "news" styling.
  - Medical advice beyond basic first-response guidance.
  - Political framing.
  - Topics where the safety facts are still unverified at script lock.

## 5. How this differs from the reference (originality statement)

| Reference | Worldosphere |
|---|---|
| One location and one protagonist across a multi-part serial | Daily standalone scenarios with a rotating cast of four. A serial only as a measured test |
| Zombie fiction only (in the sample) | Three pillars, including fact-checked real disasters |
| Mid-episode "Farm Rule" cards | The Fork (decision split), the Sphere (invisible threat dome) and the Verdict (outcome card) |
| Ends on a cliffhanger + "follow" | Ends on a resolution + Verdict; an optional one-line forward hook |
| Caps-only condensed captions with a drop shadow | Sentence-case humanist captions in ink tags; speaker chips; mono HUD |
| Round dot-faced figures | Bean-head "inked diorama" characters with brows and two-tone shading |

The *techniques* learned from the reference and kept as principles:
- Personal-relevance hooks.
- Mechanism graphics.
- A ladder of micro-payoffs.
- Silence as punctuation.
- No gore.
- Pacing matched to the beat.

Everything else is original.
