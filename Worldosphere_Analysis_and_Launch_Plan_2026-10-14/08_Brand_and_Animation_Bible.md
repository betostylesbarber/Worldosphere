## 1. Three creative directions considered

| | **A. "The Fork" — decision-driven survival scenarios** | **B. "Field Notes" — a survivor's notebook anthology** | **C. "Outbreak Atlas" — map-first strategy simulations** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Core idea | Each episode is a self-contained *scenario* in an ordinary place, built around one visible decision and its consequence. A recurring cast of four plays different scenarios | Each episode is narrated from a survivor's handwritten notebook in a paper-craft style, slow and intimate | A stylised globe/map interface simulates the spread of a threat; characters are small icons; the decision is strategic |
| Fit with verified niche (05) | **High.** Keeps the reference's strongest principles (personal relevance, mechanism graphics, micro-payoffs) with a fresh device | Medium. Strong mood, but lower immediacy; risk of slow openings | Medium-low. Drifts toward geography and strategy; weak character attachment; the brief warns against redirecting to geography |
| Hook strength (frame 0) | High: an in-motion cold open in a recognisable place | Medium: paper textures read as "calm" | Medium: maps need decoding |
| Production feasibility (daily) | **High** with reusable rigs and kits | Medium: hand-drawn texture work per episode | High for maps, low for character scenes |
| Distinctiveness vs reference | High (Fork / Sphere / Verdict, new cast and style) | High | High |
| Risk | Formula fatigue if the Fork appears identically every time | Too slow for Shorts; text-heavy | Feels like a game UI, not a story |
| **Decision** | **CHOSEN** | Rejected as the main system; its "notebook" texture is reused only for Verdict-card paper | Rejected as the main system; its map grammar survives inside the Sphere signature |

**Why A:** It is the only direction that serves all three pillars, scales to a daily cadence through reusable assets, and puts a *decision*, the thing people discuss and share, at the centre.

## 2. Channel promise and personality
- **Promise:** "Worldosphere drops you into the first minutes of a disaster — zombie or real — and shows the one decision that decides who makes it."
- **Personality:** calm under pressure, sharp, quietly funny, never smug. A field instructor who has seen things and respects the viewer's intelligence.
- **Never:** hype, gore, mockery of victims, false certainty, fake urgency, political or tragedy-bait framing.

## 3. Narrative voice and emotional tone
- **Narrator ("the Instructor"):** speaks to *you* in the hook, then follows a named cast member. Short declarative sentences. One idea per sentence. Concrete nouns. Present tense for scenarios ("Kai has three blocks to go"). Past tense only for the final turn of a story.
- **Emotional curve per episode:** *Unease → Choice → Consequence → Relief or dread → Takeaway.*
- **Humour:** dry, character-based, at most one beat per episode, never at a victim's expense.
- **Tense rule for real disasters:** fictional characters, real physics. The narrator never says "you will survive if…". It says what the guidance is and why.

## 4. Characters (original cast)

All human characters share one construction so rigs, poses and crowds are reusable.

### 4.1 Shared construction ("inked diorama" figures)
- **Head:** a bean/capsule shape, taller than wide (about 1 : 1.2), not a circle. **Eyebrows always present** (the main emotion carrier). Eyes are small ovals with a single highlight. The mouth has 6 shapes (rest, O, E, wide, frown, tight line).
- **Body:** a tapered torso shape. Limbs are **"noodle" tubes with taper**, two bones each (upper/lower), with soft elbows.
- **Hands:** mittens with a separate thumb, plus 4 hand poses (open, fist, point, grip).
- **Proportions:** adults 5 heads tall; Kai 4.6; Mia 3.4.
- **Line:** 6 px ink outline at 1080×1920 (#161A22). Interior detail lines 3 px.
- **Shading:** flat base + **one** shadow tone (multiply 30%, ink-tinted) on the side away from the key light. One rim-light stroke (2–4 px) from the scene's practical light at night.
- **Silhouette test:** every character must be identifiable as a solid black silhouette at 120 px tall.

**Anthology rule:** each episode is a separate *scenario*. The cast keep their identities and skills, but events don't carry over unless an episode is marked SAGA. No cast member is shown killed. Losses happen to guest characters, off-screen or implied. **Mia is never shown harmed** (brand safety rule).

### 4.3 Performance rules
- **Key poses first** (anticipation → action → follow-through → settle). Minimum 3 distinct key poses per character per shot over 1.5 s.
- **Reactions arrive 4–6 frames after the stimulus**, with the eyes leading the head.
- **One clear action per shot.** A character either moves or reacts, not both at full intensity.
- **Gaze tells the viewer where to look.** Before a cut to a reveal, the character looks toward it.
- **Lip-sync:** 6-shape phoneme sets for spoken lines. Off-screen lines play over a *closed-mouth* listener (fixes REF01's attribution issue).
- **Hands and objects:** contact points pinned (no sliding). The prop is parented to the hand for the whole hold.
- **Feet:** planted frames locked. Walk and run cycles at 12 fps drawn on twos inside a 24 fps timeline (12 §6).

### 4.4 Guest characters and crowds
- A **guest rig** with swappable heads (6), hair (8), tops (8) and colourways builds crowds and one-off characters.
- Crowds: 3 depth bands (foreground 1–3 figures, midground 5–12, background ≤40 as simplified silhouettes). Cycle offsets are random, 3–11 frames.

## 5. The infected ("the Turned") and other threats

### 5.1 Design (original; no gore)
- Same base rig as the humans, so any human can become Turned.
- **Pose shift:** head tilted forward and down 15–20°, one shoulder dropped, arms slack, weight on the heels.
- **Skin:** desaturated toward grey-green (#8A9A86 overlay at 60%).
- **Eyes:** solid pale ovals, no pupils (#E9EFD8), no glow.
- **Clothes:** the person's own clothes, scuffed. **No wounds, no blood sprays, no visible bites.** Bites are implied (a torn sleeve, a bandage) or off-screen.
- **Motion:** a 14-frame shamble on twos. They never sprint. When a target is visible, a "lurch" at 1.4× walk speed.
- **Tell (WR-02):** **they don't breathe.** No breath fog, no chest motion, no misted glass.

### 5.2 Real-disaster "antagonists"
Physics, never monsters. Water, fire, wind, cold, pressure and crowds are drawn as **forces with readable direction**: arrows, flow lines, the Sphere. Never as faces or creatures.

## 6. Colour, line, texture, light, depth

### 6.1 Palette (hex)
| Token | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Ink | #161A22 | Outlines, darkest values, caption tag fill (at 72%) |
| Night Teal | #1F3A44 | Night shadows, night sky base |
| Dusk Slate | #3D5560 | Mid shadows, distant layers |
| Fog | #9FB3B6 | Atmosphere, far layers |
| Paper | #F2EBDD | Cards, Verdict paper, daylight highlights |
| **Worldosphere Amber** (brand) | **#F5A524** | Brand accent, the sound Sphere, resources, clues |
| **Signal Red** (threat only) | **#E5483B** | Threat Spheres, danger words, infected zones. Never decorative |
| Cold Cyan | #4FC3D9 | Water, cold, pressure Spheres |
| Safe Green | #6BBF59 | Checks, safe zones, survived |
| Gas Violet | #9B7FD1 | Invisible gas / CO Spheres |

**Contrast hierarchy (per frame):** 1 focal subject at the highest value contrast. Threat accent (red) appears in **≤8% of frame area**, except on alert cards. Backgrounds sit ≥2 value steps away from characters (squint test). Night scenes use teal shadows and amber practical lights. Daylight scenes use paper highlights and slate shadows.

### 6.2 Line and texture
- Ink outlines on characters and key props. Backgrounds use **no outlines** (shape-only) for automatic depth separation.
- A light paper grain overlay (3% opacity, static, not boiling) across the frame for brand texture. It is compression-safe because it is static and low contrast.

### 6.3 Light
- One key light per scene (warm practical at night, cool sky by day) plus ink-tinted shadows. **No time-of-day jump without a visible cause** (a clock, sunset, lights switching on).

### 6.4 Depth
- 4 planes: foreground silhouettes (darkest, 8–12 px blur only at ≤10% of frame), subject plane (sharp), midground (−1 value step), background (−2 steps, fog tint).
- Parallax ratios: foreground 1.6×, subject 1.0×, midground 0.6×, background 0.3×.

## 7. Camera and animation behaviour
- **Default camera:** locked off, or slow drift (≤2% frame width per second). Push-ins for realisations. **No constant zooming, no shake** except physical impacts (≤6 frames, ≤12 px amplitude).
- **Shot length targets (MEASURED inspiration, adapted):**
  - Explainer beats 0.8–1.6 s.
  - Story beats 1.5–3 s.
  - Suspense holds 2.5–5 s with internal motion.
  - Average per episode about 1.6–2.2 s.
- **Frame rate:** 24 fps project; characters on twos (12 drawings/s) for the hand-made feel; camera and Sphere on ones.
- **Screen direction:** threats enter from screen-left or the background; escapes move screen-right or toward camera. Keep it consistent within an episode.
- **Transitions:** hard cuts by default, plus the three branded transitions:
  - The Fork split.
  - The Sphere expand-to-cut, where the dome wipes to the next shot.
  - The Verdict paper slide.
  - Thematic match wipes (water rises into a diagram) are allowed once per episode.

## 8. The three Worldosphere signatures

### 8.1 THE FORK — the visible decision
- **What:** At the central decision the frame **freezes**, an amber crack runs diagonally (top-right to bottom-left, 12° from vertical) and splits the frame into two panels. Each panel shows a 0.4–0.8 s preview of one option with a ≤3-word label in a Big Shoulders Display chip (e.g. "DOOR" / "WINDOW"). After the hold (1.0–1.6 s total), the chosen panel **wipes to full frame** and the other desaturates and slides away.
- **Why (purpose):** Makes the key decision explicit, invites the viewer to choose before the answer (active viewing), and gives comment debates a clear reference ("I'd have picked left").
- **Rules:** Once per episode (twice only in "Both Endings"). Labels ≤3 words. Never fake: the unchosen option's consequence must be plausible and, for Real Rule episodes, fact-checked.
- **Sound:** a dry "paper tear" whoosh on the split; a soft tick when one side is chosen.

### 8.2 THE SPHERE — invisible forces made visible
- **What:** A translucent 3/4-perspective dome drawn *inside the scene* around a source (sound, water rise, heat, smoke/gas, crowd pressure, radiation) with a dotted rim, a distance tick ("≈100 M") in JetBrains Mono, and colour by type (Amber sound · Cyan water/cold · Red direct threat · Violet gas). Whoever is *inside* the dome is affected. Domes expand on a 0.6–1.0 s ease-out.
- **Why:** Comprehension of cause and effect in about 2 s, with a brand motif ("-sphere").
- **Rules:** Only when the force matters to a decision. Distances must be plausible (claim ledger). Never more than 2 domes on screen.

### 8.3 THE VERDICT — every episode resolves
- **What:** In the last 2–4 s, a paper card slides in over the final image (not over black). It shows the **status** (SURVIVED · ESCAPED · SAFE · TRAPPED · LOST · UNKNOWN; SAFE is used when the danger was avoided rather than escaped, mostly in Real Rule episodes), the **decisive factor** in ≤8 words, and, on Real Rule episodes, a **REAL RULE** line (fact-checked) with "Sources in description".
- **Why:** Satisfying closure, a quotable takeaway, trust, and a consistent end ritual that tells new viewers what the channel is.
- **Rules:** Always truthful to the story. Never "Follow to find out". A forward hook, if any, is one spoken line *after* the Verdict, ≤1.5 s.

**Principles learned from the analysis vs original additions**

| Principle (from analysis) | Worldosphere's original execution |
|---|---|
| Mechanism graphics explain invisible causes (F02) | The Sphere: in-scene domes, colour-typed, ledger-checked |
| Decision and consequence drive interest (F01, F03) | The Fork: a frozen split showing both options |
| Takeaway cards are quotable (F04) | The Verdict: an end-of-episode outcome + factor + REAL RULE |
| Silence punctuates (F07) | One planned "drop" per episode, paired with a visual hold |
| Story-tied endings (F08) | Resolution first; an optional forward hook after it |

## 10. Voice direction and sound identity
- **Narrator:** warm, low-mid register, conversational authority ("a calm park ranger on a radio"). Pace 160–175 wpm with planned pauses (0.4–0.8 s) before reveals. No vocal fry or announcer cadence. **Do not imitate any identifiable creator's voice.** Options: the creator's own voice; a hired actor with a buyout licence; or a licensed synthetic voice on a commercial plan, with no cloning of real people (22).
- **Characters:** short lines (≤7 words) in distinct registers (4.2).
- **Sound identity:**
  - A two-note **Worldosphere motif**: an amber "tick-tock" played on muted marimba + low felt piano, 0.8 s, used under the Verdict only.
  - Ambiences are always present (room tone, rain, wind, crowd walla).
  - The music bed is a sparse pulse (70–90 BPM) that drops out once per episode.
  - Infected: no growls. Use cloth rustle, slow shuffles and a wet click of the jaw (subtle). Their silence is part of the dread.
- **Mix targets:** dialogue −16 to −18 LUFS short-term under the music bed (bed about 14–18 dB under voice in gaps), integrated −14 LUFS ±1, true peak ≤ −1.0 dBTP. These are convention, not a YouTube requirement (B §3).

## 11. Opening and ending conventions
- **Opening (0–3 s):**
  - Frame 0 already contains the protagonist and the anomaly.
  - The hook card (≤6 words, top safe zone) appears on frame 0.
  - The first spoken word lands ≤0.3 s.
  - No logo sting.
  - A small "SCENARIO 0NN" tag (JetBrains Mono 30 px, 60% opacity) top-left for the first 2.0 s.
- **Ending:**
  - The Verdict over the final image.
  - The motif.
  - An optional ≤1.5 s forward line.
  - The last frame should visually rhyme with frame 0 where natural, for a clean loop (never a forced loop).

## 12. Visual quality standard (acceptance and rejection)

**Accept a shot only if all apply:**
1. Clear focal point. The subject silhouette reads at phone size (360 px wide preview).
2. Character proportions match the turnaround (±3%).
3. At least 2 expressive key poses per action. No "floating" holds longer than 1 s without breathing or blinking motion.
4. Colour follows the palette. Red only on threats and alerts.
5. Background ≥2 value steps from the subject. Fog on far planes.
6. Depth planes as specified. Parallax direction consistent.
7. Contacts: feet locked on planted frames, props parented to hands, objects stay where they were put.
8. Scene geography: threat position and exits consistent across cuts (keep a top-down floor plan for every location).
9. Effects (smoke, rain, Sphere) support the story. Effects stay under 25% of the frame unless the effect *is* the story.
10. Edges are clean at 1080×1920. Text is rendered as text (never baked in by a generator).
11. Compression: no fine high-frequency noise over large areas. Gradients carry a 1–2% grain to avoid banding.
12. Brand: palette, outline weight, caption system and the Verdict card are present.

**Reject on sight:**
- Broken or reversed limbs, stretched joints.
- Costume or hair changes between cuts.
- Sliding feet, floating props.
- Objects disappearing.
- Inconsistent threat positions.
- Flicker or boiling textures.
- Unreadable faces at phone size.
- Visual noise competing with the subject.
- Captions covering faces, hands or the threat.
- Time-of-day jumps without cause.
- Generated text artefacts.
- Gore.
