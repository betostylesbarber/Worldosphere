## 1. Final concept and hook
- **Concept:** a real survival rule told as a 60-second story. A car that goes into water floats only briefly. Water pressure pins the doors shut, so the way out is a side window, early. Owen's instinct (the door) fails, and the window works.
- **Selected hook (HP02, mid-event cold open with a real clock):** frame 0 is a *waterline split*. Above the line: rain and the car roof. Below: the nose-down car, headlights glowing, Owen's face lit by the dash. T1 reads "≈1:00". Spoken: "When a car goes into water, you get about one minute." H1: **ABOUT ONE MINUTE**.
- **Why it wins:** the danger, the stakes and the time limit are clear in under 3 s. The clock promises a specific answer (what to do in that minute). The facts are verified (C-201–C-204).
- **Alternatives considered:**
  - HP04 wrong instinct: "Your car is sinking. Your first instinct is the door. That's the mistake." Strong, but it spends the opening on a negative.
  - HP10 split comparison: door vs window side by side from frame 0. Clear, but less urgent.

- **Door shove (05):** the body pushes and the door stays rigid; the shove recoils Owen (follow-through).
- **Pressure arrows (06):** 7 arrows evenly over the door panel; inward pulse; L1 "WATER PRESSURE".
- **Glass burst (09):** craze lines spread from the lower corner in 3 frames, the pane drops in 8 frames, water spray plus rain enters.
- **Exit (10):** head and shoulders out, then a kick. Hands contact the window frame (pinned); no clipping through the door.
- **Drop (12):** a still camera, headlights dim over 2 s, bubbles rise across frame. No caption.
