# 04 — Cross-video findings and evidence ledger

Scope: the two supplied videos, REF01 and REF02 (Parts 2 and 1 of "The Last Farm"). Timestamps are in seconds. Every finding answers the seven required questions. Findings about *why* something works are labelled INFERRED: with two videos and no metrics, causation cannot be established.

---

## F01 — The "conditional instruction" hook that turns into a named story
1. **What happens:** The narrator opens in the second person with a conditional and a counter-intuitive order: "If the zombie apocalypse starts while you're on a farm, don't run to town." About 12 s later the "you" becomes a named character ("Eli's first instinct is to drive into town…").
2. **Where:** REF02 0.1–4.0 (hook), 12.3 (switch to third person), 15.7 ("Don't." stop beat).
3. **Purpose (INFERRED):** The second person creates personal relevance: *what would I do?* The order ("don't") creates a specific curiosity gap. The named character gives the viewer someone to follow and lets the story show consequences without blaming "you".
4. **Evidence:** OBSERVED in the transcript and captions. The premise is clear by about 3 s (§3 of the REF02 breakdown).
5. **Worldosphere reproduction:** Keep the *structure*: personal-relevance opening, then a named stand-in from the recurring cast. Change the *surface*. Worldosphere openings are **in-motion cold opens** (the event has already started in frame 0), with a ≤6-word hook card and a first spoken line that asks or contradicts. See hook patterns H1–H12 in `14_Hook_Retention_Conversion_Playbook.md`.
6. **Improvement:** Frame 0 of REF02 is a calm kitchen with no text (MEASURED: the first caption appears at 0.07 s; the alert visual at 1.9 s). Put the anomaly *in* frame 0.
7. **Evaluation:** YouTube "Stayed to watch / Viewed vs swiped away" plus retention at 3 s, compared across hook patterns at similar video age (see 21).

## F02 — Mechanism shots: invisible cause and effect drawn as diagrams, maps and counters
1. **What:** Short infographic shots show *how* a risk works: a well-pump power chain, sound rings with distances, a converging-roads map, threat dots moving toward the farm over "1/6/10 MIN ENGINE ON", a perimeter line with "1 KM OF FENCE".
2. **Where:** REF02 7.85–9.13 (blueprint), 16.67–19.37 (town map), 23.95–26.73 (well diagram), 31.97–33.90 (noise rings), 43.30–46.17 (animal noise rings). REF01 43.70–47.80 (threat map), 51.20–53.37 (OFF state), 61.47–63.57 (fence break), 78.50–83.90 (perimeter).
3. **Purpose (INFERRED):** Comprehension at speed. A viewer understands *why* in about 2–3 s without extra narration. The diagrams also make the logic feel credible.
4. **Evidence:** OBSERVED, 9 such shots. MEASURED: diagram shots are among the longest in REF02 (the well diagram is 2.78 s against a 1.07 s median), which suggests deliberate dwell time.
5. **Worldosphere reproduction:** The **Sphere** signature (08 §5). Every invisible force (sound, water, heat, gas, crowd pressure, radiation) is drawn as a translucent 3/4-perspective dome *inside the scene*, with a distance tick, not as a separate top-down map. Use maps sparingly.
6. **Improvement:** Use mechanism shots only where the numbers are right. REF02's supply card shows "WATER 73 GAL ✓" next to 12 cattle and a horse, which is a factual error (F12). Each Sphere is checked against the claim ledger.
7. **Evaluation:** Look for flat or rising retention through mechanism beats rather than dips. Also count comment corrections per 1,000 views (target: near zero on factual points).

## F03 — A ladder of micro-payoffs
1. **What:** Small questions are raised and answered within seconds while a larger question escalates.
2. **Where:** REF01: "Eli?" (0.6) → "It was Mara" (5.6). "Covered in blood" (11.4) → "Not bitten" card (25.1). "It's not my blood" (26.5) → backstory (30–37). "So did they" (40.1) → threat map (43.7). Then two escalating reveals: the infected is inside (68–73) and the horde (75–78).
3. **Purpose (INFERRED):** Each answer rewards attention and each new question keeps it.
4. **Evidence:** OBSERVED timing. MEASURED: REF01 has 12 pauses longer than 0.4 s, clustered around the reveals.
5. **Worldosphere reproduction:** Story template rule. Every 5–8 s, a beat must **reveal, change, escalate or resolve**, and at least two questions must be resolved before the final reveal (12 §Script).
6. **Improvement:** End on a resolution rather than a cliffhanger, so each Short satisfies on its own (F08).
7. **Evaluation:** No unexplained dips in the retention graph. Low "confused" comment rate.

## F04 — Recurring rule cards
1. **What:** Numbered cards ("FARM RULE #1 — STAY AWAY FROM POPULATION CENTERS"; "#2 — NOISE TRAVELS FARTHER THAN YOU THINK"; "#3 — CHECK FIRST. TRUST SECOND.").
2. **Where:** REF02 18.67, 35.53; REF01 18.8–21.4 (the card stays on screen across three cuts).
3. **Purpose (INFERRED):** Turns fiction into portable takeaways that are quotable and shareable. The numbering implies a collectable series.
4. **Evidence:** OBSERVED.
5. **Worldosphere reproduction (original):** The **Verdict** card at the *end* of each episode (outcome + decisive factor). On real-disaster episodes it adds a **REAL RULE** line that is fact-checked and sourced. We do not copy the mid-episode rule cards.
6. **Improvement:** Rules must be true when presented as real advice, and fiction rules must be labelled as fiction.
7. **Evaluation:** Shares per 1,000 views on Real Rule episodes compared with Outbreak episodes. Watch the comments for people quoting the rule.

## F05 — Caption system: dense, short, semantically coloured
1. **What:** Condensed all-caps captions with a dark shadow, centred at 62–65% of frame height. Each group shows for about 1 s and holds about 3 words. Red marks threats, yellow marks resources and clues. Captions are dropped when a graphic already carries the words.
2. **Where:** Throughout both videos. MEASURED: REF02 has 51 groups (mean 1.04 s, 3.4 words, max 5). REF01 has 68 groups (mean 1.12 s, 3.1 words). There are 8 and 13 emphasised groups respectively.
3. **Purpose (INFERRED):** Watchable with the sound off. Words arrive in sync with speech. Colour primes the meaning of each word.
4. **Evidence:** MEASURED (caption-band analysis, all 261 change events).
5. **Worldosphere reproduction (original design):** A **sentence-case** humanist caption in a soft ink "tag" box (Atkinson Hyperlegible Next, OFL). Speaker chips identify dialogue. The colour semantics are similar but drawn from a different palette (09).
6. **Improvement:** Fix speech/caption mismatches ("SHE'D CROSSED" against spoken "She crossed"; "THAT'S NOT ALL" against "It's not all"). Avoid orphan line breaks ("…THE ONLY / ONE"). Identify speakers.
7. **Evaluation:** A QA check that captions are word-for-word, a muted comprehension test (09 §12), and an A/B pattern comparison of sentence case against caps (21).

## F06 — Pacing matched to content type
1. **What:** Part 1, an explainer montage, is fast. Part 2, a dramatic dialogue, is slower.
2. **Where (MEASURED):** REF02: mean shot 1.39 s (median 1.07), 194 wpm while speaking, speech on 96% of the time, 2 pauses over 0.4 s. REF01: mean shot 2.42 s (median 2.10), 171 wpm, speech on 84% of the time, 12 pauses. REF01's longest shot (7.2 s) is the slow reveal in the grass.
3. **Purpose (INFERRED):** Information beats need momentum. Suspense beats need held frames and silence.
4. **Evidence:** MEASURED.
5. **Worldosphere reproduction:** Recipe-specific pacing targets (12 §Edit). Explainer beats run 0.8–1.6 s per shot. Suspense beats run 2–5 s with motion inside the shot. Target voice rate is 160–175 wpm with planned pauses.
6. **Improvement:** Do not push Part-1 speed (194 wpm) into every beat. Plan "breath" pauses before reveals.
7. **Evaluation:** Retention slope through dense explainer passages against suspense passages.

## F07 — Silence and slowed delivery as punctuation
1. **What:** The music bed is pulled to near silence for single lines, and the narrator slows down on final lines.
2. **Where (MEASURED):** REF01 gaps fall to −51/−47 dBFS around "So did they." (39.5–41.7 s) and to −53 dBFS after "Silence." (50.6–51.3 s), against a typical bed of −26 to −34 dBFS. REF02's final sentence is delivered at 1.4 words/s (average 3.3). REF01's final sentence is at 2.3 words/s.
3. **Purpose (INFERRED):** Contrast makes the key lines land.
4. **Evidence:** MEASURED (gap RMS, word timing).
5. **Worldosphere reproduction:** One planned "drop" per episode, a cue-sheet rule (12 §Sound). We do not use a generic riser before every reveal.
6. **Improvement:** Add a matching *visual* stillness (a held frame) to the audio drop so the drop also reads with the sound off.
7. **Evaluation:** The retention graph around the drop: no dip, or a bump.

## F08 — Cliffhanger endings with story-tied follow CTAs
1. **What:** Both parts end on an unresolved threat, then a black typographic card ("PART 2 — SOMETHING IS IN THE BARN — FOLLOW TO SURVIVE DAY 1"; "PART 3 — SURVIVE THE NIGHT — THE FINALE — FOLLOW TO SEE WHO MAKES IT TO SUNRISE").
2. **Where:** REF02 54.1–61.0; REF01 70.9–87.1. The end cards hold for 1.7 s (REF02) and 3.2 s (REF01).
3. **Purpose (INFERRED):** Turns the open question into a reason to follow.
4. **Evidence:** OBSERVED. **UNKNOWN:** whether this raised follows or hurt satisfaction. No data.
5. **Worldosphere reproduction:** **Standalone resolution first**, then an optional one-line forward hook. Multi-part sagas run only as a measured test (Days 15–16).
6. **Improvement:** Never make following the price of an ending. The Verdict card gives every episode a clear outcome.
7. **Evaluation:** For the saga test, compare follows from Part 1, retention on Part 2, and Part-2 views relative to Part 1 (21).

## F09 — Continuity bridge between parts
1. **What:** Part 2 opens on Part 1's exact final composition (dark barn doors, silhouette in the foreground).
2. **Where:** REF02 57.6–59.3 equals REF01 0.0–1.87.
3. **Purpose (INFERRED):** Instant re-entry for returning viewers.
4. **Evidence:** OBSERVED.
5. **Worldosphere reproduction:** Used only in sagas. For standalone episodes the equivalent is a *recurring cast and visual grammar*, not a recap.
6. **Improvement:** Add a 1-second, in-world "previously" for new viewers (for example the protagonist's line restating the situation).
7. **Evaluation:** Part-2 3-second retention against the channel average.

## F10 — Character economy
1. **What:** A silent protagonist (Eli never speaks). A dog acts as the comic relief, the early warning and the emotional anchor. A new character is introduced with a name callout. Dialogue lines are 1–6 words.
2. **Where:** REF02 39.9–43.3 (dog comedy), 54.3–55.9 (dog alert). REF01 5.5–8.3 (name callout), 12.6–16.2 (dialogue), 63.6–66.0 and 73.2–73.8 (dog warning).
3. **Purpose (INFERRED):** Keeps production cheap (little lip-sync) while creating attachment.
4. **Evidence:** OBSERVED, plus MEASURED voice pitch showing a male narrator and a female character voice.
5. **Worldosphere reproduction:** A recurring **cast of four** with distinct silhouettes and skills (08 §4). The protagonist may speak short lines. We do **not** copy the dog device. Worldosphere's "animal tell" is replaced by character skills (a lineman who knows the grid, a driver who knows loading docks, a teen who notices details).
6. **Improvement:** Give the protagonist one clear *decision* per episode. Eli mostly reacts.
7. **Evaluation:** Comments that name the characters. Repeat viewers (returning-viewer metric).

## F11 — Visual design built for phone readability
1. **What:** Flat vector shapes with soft gradients and simulated depth of field. Large silhouettes, red reserved for threats, volumetric light, and fog layers. No gore: blood appears only as stains.
2. **Where:** Throughout both videos.
3. **Purpose (INFERRED):** Readable at phone size, tonally serious without gore, and advertiser-safe.
4. **Evidence:** OBSERVED. The YouTube policy context (B §8) is that animated violence is generally not removed, but dwelling on gore risks age restriction.
5. **Worldosphere reproduction (original):** An "inked diorama" style: bold ink outlines, bean-shaped heads with brows, a two-tone cel shade, and a teal/amber night palette (08 §6).
6. **Improvement:** Keep the time of day consistent (F13). Use brows for clearer facial emotion than dot faces give.
7. **Evaluation:** Phone-size squint test (11 §Acceptance) and comments mentioning confusion.

## F12 — Factual looseness inside a "practical" frame
1. **What:** The video presents survival logistics as practical, but some numbers fail a check.
2. **Where:** REF02 49.4–53.8: "WATER 73 GAL ✓", "on paper… weeks" with 30 hens, 12 cattle, a horse and a dog. Extension-service figures give roughly 110–183 gal per day, so 73 gal lasts under a day. REF01 8.3–10.2: Mara follows the generator over "three miles", but spherical spreading alone takes a 57–73 dB(A)@7 m generator down to about 0–16 dB(A) at 4.8 km.
3. **Purpose:** None. These are defects (the "on paper" line may be meant as irony, but the ✓ contradicts that).
4. **Evidence:** MEASURED arithmetic using sourced inputs (claims C-020 and C-021 in 07).
5. **Worldosphere reproduction:** Not applicable. This is what we avoid.
6. **Improvement:** A claim ledger for every number on screen, and fiction rules labelled as fiction.
7. **Evaluation:** Comment-correction rate. A QA gate in which no unverified number ships.

## F14 — A diegetic UI as the inciting device
1. **What:** A phone emergency alert ("SHELTER IN PLACE / AVOID POPULATED AREAS") delivers the inciting incident inside the world.
2. **Where:** REF02 1.73–4.27.
3. **Purpose (INFERRED):** Believability. Viewers know what these alerts look like.
4. **Evidence:** OBSERVED.
5. **Worldosphere reproduction:** Use original in-world devices that vary by episode: a delivery manifest, a dashboard warning, a radio, a smoke alarm, a weather siren. Do not reuse a phone alert as a default opener. Fictional alerts must not imitate the real Wireless Emergency Alert layout closely enough to be mistaken for one (the YouTube context requirement, B §8).
6. **Improvement:** Show the alert *with* the contradiction in the same frame (the Day 1 hook).
7. **Evaluation:** 3-second retention by opener type.

## F15 — Time pressure through counters and time compression
1. **What:** A "29:57 LEFT" timer jumps between shots (time compression). Counters tick up ("WATER STORED 3 → 73 GAL"). The engine-time pill reads "1 → 6 → 10 MIN".
2. **Where:** REF02 19.37–23.95; REF01 43.70–47.80.
3. **Purpose (INFERRED):** Urgency and a sense of progress.
4. **Evidence:** OBSERVED.
5. **Worldosphere reproduction:** A HUD **clock pill** (JetBrains Mono, tabular digits) used only when time is a real constraint, as on Day 2 (about 60 s to escape a car).
6. **Improvement:** Never show a false countdown. The time on screen must match the story and the facts.
7. **Evaluation:** The QA check, and the claim ledger for real-world timings.
