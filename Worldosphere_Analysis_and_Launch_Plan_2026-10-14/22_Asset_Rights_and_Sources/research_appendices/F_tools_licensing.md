# F. Tools and Licensing Research: Flat-Vector 2D Cutout Shorts Studio

**Access date for every source below: 2026-10-14.**
**Scope:** a solo or small team making one YouTube Short (1080x1920) a day. Characters are stick-limbed and round-headed with rigged limbs. Scenes use parallax backgrounds, infographic overlays (maps, timers, counters) and burned-in captions. Narration is a human voice or a licensed synthetic voice.

## How this was researched (read first)

- **Web search only.** Direct page fetches (WebFetch and curl) failed during this session because of a DNS error and an egress-proxy block. Every fact tagged with a URL comes from search-engine result summaries of that URL, not from reading the page myself. Treat prices especially as "seen in search results on 2026-10-14". They may be stale, regional or promotional, and all of them may change.
- **Search budget ran out before sections 6 and 7 were finished.** Items tagged **[PK]** come from prior knowledge (cutoff mid-2026) and were **not re-verified** on 2026-10-14. Check them before relying on them.
- **No prices were invented.** Where no price was found, the table says so.

**Takeaways for section 1**
- For **dedicated cutout rigging with lip-sync**, Moho Pro 14 gives the most for its one-time US$399.99.
- For **infographic-heavy Shorts**, AE with Duik Ángela (free) and RubberHose 3 is the most flexible. RubberHose matches stick limbs best.
- **Avoid starting on Adobe Animate** (maintenance mode) or Synfig (no stable release since 2024).

**Content ID false-claim risk.** Content ID matches audio fingerprints and cannot read a license. A paid or royalty-free license therefore **does not prevent claims**; it only helps you win a dispute. False claims come from stolen uploads, wrong composer credits or matching errors, and some composers say they are getting harder to reverse. Practical rules:

1. Keep a per-video asset log (track ID, source, license PDF or certificate, download date).
2. Safelist the channel with subscription libraries.
3. Dispute with proof, but don't dispute recklessly; disputes can escalate.
4. Use the Audio Library or your own music for evergreen uploads.

Sources: https://www.foximusic.com/blog/content-id-music-guide-monetization/ ; https://www.free-stock-music.com/article.unveiling-the-issue-of-fake-copyright-claims-on-youtube.html (accessed 2026-10-14).

**What the OFL allows for burned-in video text [PK].** The OFL governs the **font software**, not what you make with it. The OFL FAQ states that documents, images and other output created with OFL fonts are not themselves subject to the OFL. Burning OFL-licensed text into a commercial, monetized YouTube video is therefore permitted. No attribution is required in the video, and no fee is due. The OFL restricts:

- (a) selling the font files by themselves;
- (b) distributing modified fonts under a Reserved Font Name;
- (c) distributing the fonts under a different license.

If you **ship project files** that bundle the fonts (e.g. selling AE templates), include the OFL.txt and copyright notice. Verify at https://openfontlicense.org/ofl-faq/ (not re-fetched 2026-10-14).
